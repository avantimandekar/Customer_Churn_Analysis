# Customer_Churn_Analysis
Subscription-based businesses face customer churn, which leads to recurring revenue loss. Understanding which customer segments have higher churn, how customer engagement relates to cancellations, and what reasons customers give for leaving can help businesses take targeted actions to improve retention.

Data Overview - 
4,200 customer subscription records. 30,685 monthly customer activity records. 

Key Business Insights -
1) Overall Customer Churn and Revenue Loss
Out of 4,200 customers, 1,768 have cancelled their subscriptions, resulting in an overall churn rate of 42.1%. This has led to an estimated monthly recurring revenue (MRR) loss of ₹30,822. The findings highlight how customer cancellations can affect the business's monthly revenue.

2) Monthly vs Annual Subscription Performance
Monthly subscribers have a churn rate of 51.2%, compared to 26.4% for annual subscribers. They also account for approximately ₹25,296 of the total lost MRR. This indicates that monthly subscribers are more likely to leave and contribute the largest share of churn-related revenue loss.

3) Customer Tenure and Churn Patterns
Customer cancellation patterns vary depending on the subscription plan. Around 52.9% of monthly-plan customers who churn leave within their first three months. For annual subscribers, 77.1% of churn occurs between months 9 and 12. This suggests that early engagement is important for monthly subscribers, while renewal-period engagement deserves more attention for annual customers.

4) Acquisition Channel Performance
Customers acquired through promotional offers have the highest churn rate at 50.8%, whereas referral customers have the lowest at 33.8%. In terms of revenue loss, organic acquisition contributes the most, with approximately ₹9,292 in lost MRR, followed by promotional offers at ₹8,320.

5) Acquisition Channel and Subscription Plan Analysis
Monthly subscribers have higher churn rates than annual subscribers across all acquisition channels. Monthly customers acquired through promotional offers have the highest churn rate at 63.3%. In comparison, annual customers acquired through referrals have the lowest rate at 20.2%. This makes promotional monthly subscribers an important segment for retention efforts.

6) Subscription Tier Analysis
Churn rates are relatively similar across the three subscription tiers. Premium subscribers have the highest churn rate at 43.4%, followed by Basic at 42.1% and Plus at 41.5%. This suggests that subscription tier alone may not be a major factor behind customer cancellations.

7) First-Month Engagement and Churn
Customers who did not complete any workouts during their first month have a churn rate of 59.0%. This drops to 21.7% among customers who completed eight or more workouts. The pattern is consistent across monthly and annual plans, showing the importance of building customer engagement from the beginning.

8) Customer Engagement and Support Interactions
Retained customers are more active than churned customers. On average, retained customers complete 5.22 workouts and book 0.84 classes per recorded activity month. For churned customers, the averages are 3.10 workouts and 0.52 classes. Churned customers also raise slightly more support tickets (0.140 compared to 0.122). Overall, lower engagement stands out as a noticeable difference between the two groups.

9) Cancellation Reasons and Customer Engagement
Insufficient usage accounts for 41.6% of cancellations among low-engagement customers, compared with 11.7% among highly engaged customers. On the other hand, price-related cancellations make up 29.2% of cancellations among highly engaged customers. This suggests that retention strategies should differ depending on customer activity levels.

10) Active Customers and Potential Revenue at Risk
Based on November and December 2025 activity records, 2,293 active customers were included in the analysis. Of these, 694 completed an average of two or fewer workouts. This represents 30.3% of the analysed active customer base, with approximately ₹10,788 in associated MRR. Annual subscribers have a higher proportion of low-engagement customers (36.6%) than monthly subscribers (24.2%), making them an important group to monitor before renewal.

Business Recommendations -
Business should pay attention to customer onboarding, ensuring frequent participation in the workout and maintaining engagement among monthly customers. The business needs to pay special attention to low-engagement annual customers prior to renewal, analyzing promotional offers, and increasing subscription value to minimize price cancellations.

Conclusion -
This analysis highlights the relationship between customer engagement and subscription retention. The more actively the customers use the service, the more likely it is for them to remain with the company, while low engagement will lead to cancellations. With better onboarding, motivating customers to work out regularly, and detecting risky clients, the company can increase retention.
